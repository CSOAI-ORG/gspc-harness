# Why CSOAI matters for climate finance AI — carbon credits, green bonds, ESG

Climate finance increasingly uses AI for carbon credit verification, green bond assessment, ESG scoring. CSOAI's signed evidence layer provides verifiable measurement for these AI components.

## The climate finance AI landscape

Climate finance uses AI for:

- **Carbon credit verification** — MRV (measurement, reporting, verification) of carbon offsets
- **Green bond assessment** — verifying use-of-proceeds for green bonds
- **ESG scoring** — measuring companies' environmental, social, governance
- **Climate risk modeling** — physical and transition risk for portfolios
- **Carbon accounting** — Scope 1, 2, 3 emissions tracking
- **Sustainable investing** — screening funds for sustainability claims
- **Climate stress testing** — NGFS scenarios for financial institutions
- **Nature-related disclosures** — TNFD framework alignment

Each uses AI. CSOAI measures the AI components.

## Why this matters now

Several climate finance regulatory windows are open in 2026:

- **EU CSRD** (Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive) — requiring ESG disclosures for ~50,000 EU companies
- **EU Taxonomy** — defining what's "green" for sustainable finance classification
- **SFDR** (Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation) — fund-level sustainability disclosures
- **SEC Climate Disclosure** — US public company climate risk disclosures
- **ISSB IFRS S1/S2** — global sustainability disclosure standards (effective 2024-2025)
- **TNFD** (Taskforce on Nature-related Financial Disclosures) — emerging biodiversity standard

Each requires evidence of AI behavior for any AI used in climate finance. CSOAI provides that evidence.

## The greenwashing problem

Greenwashing is the biggest risk in climate finance:

- A "green" bond that funds fossil fuel projects (e.g., DWS Group scandal)
- Carbon credits that don't represent real reductions (e.g., Verra/VCS controversy)
- ESG scores that misrepresent company behavior (e.g., DWS, Deutsche Bank probes)
- AI models that systematically overstate sustainability
- Offsets that are reversed (e.g., wetland forests that burn down)

CSOAI's signed evidence helps:

1. **Verify the AI's methodology** for ESG scoring — auditable in source code
2. **Document the AI's behavior** on specific test conditions — signed cards
3. **Surface methodological errors** via corrections feed — machine-derived
4. **Provide independent third-party verification** — verifiable in any browser
5. **Enable comparison** across AI vendors and ESG providers — standardized methodology

## What CSOAI doesn't do

CSOAI doesn't measure:

- **Carbon offsets themselves** — that's a different measurement (Verifiable Carbon, Gold Standard, etc.)
- **ESG criteria themselves** — those are policy decisions (CSRC, SASB, GRI, etc.)
- **Sustainability claims themselves** — those are human judgments
- **The actual sustainability of a company/project** — that's an audit function
- **Climate science claims** — that's IPCC/peer-reviewed science
- **Whether an offset is "real"** — that's an MRV (Measurement, Reporting, Verification) function

CSOAI measures the AI components that make climate finance decisions. The AI is a tool; the decision is human.

## Why CSOAI's independence matters in climate finance

Climate finance has a particular credibility problem:

- **Carbon credit registries** (Verra, Gold Standard) earn fees per credit issued → incentive to issue more
- **ESG rating agencies** (MSCI, Sustainalytics) charge companies for ratings → incentive to be favorable
- **Big 4 consultancies** (Deloitte, PwC) charge both issuers AND investors → conflict
- **Carbon offset verifiers** earn fees per offset → incentive to overstate reductions

CSOAI's structural independence — no payment from measured entities — solves this:

- CSOAI doesn't earn fees from carbon credits issued
- CSOAI doesn't charge companies for ESG scoring
- CSOAI doesn't take money from issuers AND investors
- CSOAI doesn't earn fees from offset verification

A green bond issuer citing CSOAI's evidence can demonstrate: "Our AI verifier was independently measured by a third party with no commercial interest in the verification outcome."
