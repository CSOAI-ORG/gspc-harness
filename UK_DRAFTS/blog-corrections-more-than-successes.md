# Why CSOAI's corrections ledger has more entries than its successes

CSOAI's corrections ledger has entries for withdrawn cards, scoring rule fixes, and methodology updates. That's a feature. Corrections are the operational proof of honesty.

## The standard model

Most organisations minimise their corrections log:

- Quietly fix bugs
- Don't mention them
- Move on quickly

The result: a clean record that doesn't reflect reality.

## CSOAI's model

CSOAI publishes every correction:

- Card withdrawals
- Scoring rule fixes
- Methodology updates
- New card supersessions

The corrections feed at `councilof.ai/feeds/corrections.xml` grows over time. Every entry is a moment CSOAI got something wrong and fixed it publicly.

## Why this is honest

A clean record means:

- The organisation never makes mistakes (unlikely)
- The organisation hides its mistakes (likely)
- The organisation hasn't been used much (likely)

A corrections record with entries means:

- The organisation actually measures things
- The organisation has a process for catching errors
- The organisation is honest about what went wrong
- The organisation is trusted because of the corrections, not despite them

## Real examples

CSOAI's corrections feed has published:

- 44 swarm-axis cards withdrawn (prompt couldn't be answered wrongly)
- Reserve-attestation axis note corrected (wrong tally)

Both entries were caught by CSOAI's own review. Both were published publicly. Both improved future measurements.

## What corrections cost

Corrections cost:

1. **Reputation risk.** "CSOAI admitted an error" is harder to spin than "CSOAI fixed a bug quietly."
2. **Re-run cost.** Each correction requires re-running the affected measurements.
3. **Engineering time.** The corrections pipeline is non-trivial.
4. **Public visibility.** The corrections feed is more visible than new measurements.

CSOAI accepts all four costs because independence requires them.

## What corrections enable

Corrections enable:

1. **Trust.** A reader sees the corrections and knows the organisation is honest.
2. **Improvement.** Each correction improves future measurements.
3. **Accountability.** The corrections feed is a public record of error handling.
4. **Differentiation.** Most organisations hide errors; CSOAI publishes them.

## The metric that matters

CSOAI's most important metric is not "how many cards published" but "how many corrections published and verified."

A growth-only metric rewards measurement without error handling. A corrections-aware metric rewards both measurement and honesty.

CSOAI's corrections feed is the second metric.

## The bottom line

Corrections > silence. Errors caught and published > errors hidden. That's CSOAI's honest position.
