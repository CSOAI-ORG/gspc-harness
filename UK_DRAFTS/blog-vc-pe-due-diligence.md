# Why CSOAI matters for VC and PE due diligence — the missing evidence layer

VC and PE firms are deploying capital into AI companies. Due diligence is incomplete without independent measurement of model behavior. CSOAI provides that evidence layer.

## The current state of AI due diligence

Most VC and PE due diligence on AI companies includes:

1. **Financial:** Revenue, growth, burn rate
2. **Team:** Founders, employees, advisors
3. **Market:** TAM, competition, positioning
4. **Technical:** Architecture, stack, scalability

What's missing: **independent measurement of the AI model's behavior**.

A VC investing in an AI company needs to know:

- What does the model actually do?
- Does it match the company's claims?
- How does it perform under stress?
- Does it scale safely?

CSOAI provides signed evidence for all four.

## What CSOAI provides for VC/PE

CSOAI provides:

1. **Independent model measurement.** The VC can cite CSOAI's signed cards in their investment memo.
2. **Verifiable methodology.** CSOAI's scoring is in source code, not vendor narrative.
3. **Public corrections.** CSOAI's corrections feed documents the company's AI errors.
4. **Comparable benchmarks.** CSOAI's axes allow comparison across portfolio companies.
5. **Independent verification.** Anyone can verify CSOAI's measurements, including the VC's LPs.

## Why this matters for AI investing

AI investing has a measurement gap:

- Founders claim "our model is the best at X"
- Self-reported benchmarks are unfalsifiable
- Investors lack the time/expertise to verify
- Bad models get funded; good models get overlooked

CSOAI closes the gap:

- Founders can't game CSOAI's measurement
- Investors cite CSOAI's cards in their memos
- LPs can verify the investor's diligence
- The market gets a standardized measurement layer

## What a VC's CSOAI-based diligence looks like

A VC's CSOAI-based due diligence:

1. **Pull the company's claims.** "Our model scores 0.85 on EU AI Act risk-tier classification."
2. **Run CSOAI's harness on the model.** Get the signed card.
3. **Verify the claim.** If the card shows 0.85, claim is supported. If 0.72, claim is overstated.
4. **Cite the card in the investment memo.** "[Model] measured 0.85 on [axis] by CSOAI Ltd at [URL]. Signed card [content_id]."
5. **Subscribe to corrections feed.** If the model degrades, the VC knows first.

This is structured, verifiable diligence.

## What VCs should look for

A VC evaluating an AI investment should ask:

1. **What does the model measure on CSOAI's 22 axes?** If the company hasn't run CSOAI, that's a red flag.
2. **What corrections has CSOAI logged for this model?** If the model has 0 corrections, it's likely unmeasured.
3. **Does the company's claims match CSOAI's signed cards?** If they differ, the company is overstating.
4. **Is the company's methodology CSOAI-compatible?** If they're using a proprietary black-box, that's a higher-risk investment.

A company that runs CSOAI before raising is signaling diligence.

## What CSOAI is NOT

CSOAI is NOT:

- A VC fund
- A due diligence consultancy
- An AI score provider
- An investment recommendation

CSOAI is measurement infrastructure. VCs use it; VCs don't become CSOAI.

AI due diligence needs measurement evidence. CSOAI provides it. The investor, the founder, and the LP all benefit from the same signed cards.
