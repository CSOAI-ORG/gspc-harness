# Why CSOAI's tagline isn't "AI safety"

CSOAI's tagline is "measurement, not certification." It could be "AI safety" or "AI assurance" or "AI governance." It isn't, because those taglines are claims CSOAI doesn't make.

## What taglines do

A tagline is a one-line description of what an organisation does. Taglines are:

- The first thing a reader sees
- The phrase that gets quoted in coverage
- The mental hook for remembering the organisation

CSOAI's tagline is "measurement, not certification." It says exactly what CSOAI does and doesn't do.

## What other taglines claim

Other organisations' taglines:

- "AI safety" — claims safety, which is a regulator's role
- "AI assurance" — claims assurance, which is a buyer's judgment
- "AI governance" — claims governance, which is broader than measurement
- "AI compliance" — claims compliance, which is a specific regime
- "AI ethics" — claims ethics, which is a philosophical stance

Each of these claims something CSOAI doesn't make. Using them would be lying.

## Why "measurement" is honest

CSOAI measures:

- Specific AI systems
- On specific test conditions
- Using specific methodology
- Producing signed evidence

That's measurement. Not safety, assurance, governance, compliance, or ethics.

When a reader sees "measurement," they know exactly what to expect: signed evidence about specific test conditions.

When a reader sees "safety," they don't know what to expect: a conformity mark? a dashboard? a policy document? a self-assessment?

## Why "not certification" is honest

CSOAI doesn't certify:

- Doesn't issue conformity marks
- Doesn't say "X is safe"
- Doesn't say "X is compliant"
- Doesn't endorse

The "not certification" is explicit so readers know CSOAI isn't claiming what certifiers claim.

## What CSOAI's tagline is NOT

CSOAI's tagline is not:

- A safety claim
- An assurance claim
- A governance claim
- A compliance claim
- An ethical claim

CSOAI's tagline is a description of what CSOAI does (measurement) and what CSOAI doesn't do (certification).

## Why this matters

When an organisation's tagline is a claim, readers expect the organisation to back up the claim. When the claim is "AI safety," readers expect safety judgments. CSOAI doesn't make safety judgments.

When the tagline is a description ("measurement, not certification"), readers know what to expect. CSOAI's signed evidence matches the description.

## The trade-off

The trade-off:

- "AI safety" is a more memorable tagline
- "Measurement, not certification" is more honest

CSOAI chooses honesty over memorability. The tagline is for readers who want to know what CSOAI is. Readers who want a safety claim will go to other organisations.

## The bottom line

CSOAI's tagline says what CSOAI does and what it doesn't. That's the honest tagline.
