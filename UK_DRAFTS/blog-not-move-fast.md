# Why CSOAI rejects "move fast and break things" for AI governance

AI governance is not a "move fast" domain. The whole point is preventing harm. CSOAI's methodology is conservative, versioned, and corrections-aware.

## The Silicon Valley myth

The phrase "move fast and break things" is associated with Facebook's early growth. It captures a particular approach:

- Ship fast
- Iterate based on feedback
- Accept that some users will be unhappy
- Move on quickly

For consumer software, this approach can work. Users can leave. Competitive markets punish bad behavior.

For AI governance, this approach is **dangerous**:

- The "users" affected by AI behavior are not necessarily the people who chose the AI
- The "feedback" can be indirect (a wrongful arrest, a biased loan decision, a hallucinated medical diagnosis)
- The "unhappy users" may not be able to leave (a prisoner, a refugee, a job applicant)
- The "competitive markets" don't punish AI misbehavior — they punish the operator, who may not even know about the misbehavior

## What AI governance needs

AI governance needs:

1. **Pre-deployment evaluation.** Before the AI is deployed, we need to know what it does.
2. **Versioned methodology.** When the methodology changes, the results change. Document the change.
3. **Public correction process.** When errors are found, publish them.
4. **Independent verification.** Anyone can re-run the measurement.
5. **Refusal to certify.** "Safe" is a regulator's judgment, not a measurer's claim.

CSOAI's approach matches these requirements. "Move fast and break things" does not.

## What "move fast" looks like in AI governance

A "move fast" approach to AI governance would:

- Skip methodology versioning
- Skip public corrections
- Skip independent verification
- Skip pre-deployment evaluation
- Skip refusal to certify

The result would be: every measurement is provisional, every correction is hidden, every verification requires trust.

That's not AI governance. That's marketing.

## What "break things" looks like in AI governance

A "break things" approach to AI governance would:

- Break trust with measurers
- Break methodology by silent updates
- Break verification by hiding the keys
- Break corrections by manually editing feeds
- Break independence by accepting payment from measured entities

The result would be: a "measurement" that's actually a marketing claim. That's worse than no measurement.

## What CSOAI does instead

CSOAI does:

1. **Slow methodology versioning.** Every change is documented and reviewed.
2. **Slow public corrections.** Every correction is published, even embarrassing ones.
3. **Slow verification.** Anyone can verify, even competitors.
4. **Slow independence.** No payment from measured entities, even when revenue is needed.
5. **Slow refusal to certify.** Even when buyers want a "yes," CSOAI says "we measured X; you decide."

CSOAI is **slow on purpose**. AI governance is the domain where speed kills.

## Why CSOAI's slowness is a feature

A slow methodology:

- Is reproducible (the methodology is the same tomorrow)
- Is auditable (reviewers can read the full history)
- Is correctable (errors are surfaced, not hidden)
- Is trustworthy (the slowness is structural)

A fast methodology:

- Changes too quickly to track
- Is impossible to audit (changes happened between reviews)
- Hides errors (too quick to catch and correct)
- Loses trust (changes feel like hiding something)

## The bottom line

AI governance is not a fast domain. CSOAI is slow on purpose. That's the honest position.
