# Why CSOAI is not in the AI safety vendor market — and why that's a feature

CSOAI is positioned outside the AI safety vendor market. The platform vendors (guardrails, red-team, alignment) sell tools. CSOAI sells evidence. Different business, different incentives.

## What the AI safety vendor market sells

The AI safety vendor market sells:

- **Guardrail frameworks** — content filters, jailbreak detection, output moderation
- **Red-team platforms** — adversarial prompt generators, attack libraries
- **Alignment tools** — RLHF, DPO, constitutional AI frameworks
- **Monitoring/observability** — AI behavior dashboards, drift detection

These vendors sell tools to AI developers. Their revenue scales with AI adoption. Their incentive is to make AI safer so AI adoption continues.

## What CSOAI sells

CSOAI sells:

- **Independent measurement** of AI behavior on specific test conditions
- **Signed receipts** that bind measurements to specific runs
- **Public verification** that anyone can perform

CSOAI's revenue (when it comes) will scale with the demand for **independent evidence** about AI behavior, not with AI adoption itself.

## The conflict of interest

If CSOAI were in the AI safety vendor market, we'd have the same conflict every safety tool has:

- Buyer wants their AI to pass our tests
- Our tests measure if their AI is safe
- If we say it's unsafe, they don't buy
- If we say it's safe when it's not, real people suffer

This conflict exists for every safety tool. CSOAI solves it by **not selling safety**. We sell measurement. We don't tell buyers their AI is safe. We tell buyers what their AI did on our test.

## The structural advantage

CSOAI's structural advantage:

1. **Independence from measured entities.** No payment from them. This is structural, not contractual.
2. **Independence from safety vendors.** We don't compete with them. We measure them.
3. **Independence from AI developers.** We don't sell to them. They benefit from our existence (good measurement = better market) but we don't need their patronage.
4. **Independence from regulators.** We produce evidence. They interpret it.

This is a **trust position**, not a market position. The position is what makes the measurement credible.

## What this means commercially

CSOAI's commercial model:

- **Free data and verification** — anyone can use the board data, verify receipts
- **x402-metered tools** — $0.01 per proof for fresh measurements
- **Enterprise evidence assembly** — paid service for buyers who want curated evidence packages
- **Sponsorship** — foundations and labs sponsor CSOAI's public infrastructure

None of these involve selling a conformity mark. None involve saying "X is safe."

This is unusual for an AI governance company. Most of them sell dashboards or "safety scores." CSOAI sells **signed artifacts**.

## What CSOAI is NOT competing with

CSOAI does not compete with:

- Guardrail companies (we don't block outputs)
- Red-team companies (we don't generate attacks)
- Alignment companies (we don't train models)
- Observability companies (we don't monitor production)
- Compliance dashboards (we don't show scores)

CSOAI is orthogonal to these. We can be used alongside any of them.

## What CSOAI IS competing with

CSOAI competes with:

- **Vibes-based safety claims** — vendors who say "X is safe" without signed evidence
- **Buyer trust in their own vendors** — we offer independent verification of vendor claims
- **Self-reported metrics** — labs that publish numbers without external verification
- **Certifier regimes** — conformity marks without falsifiable measurements

In each case, CSOAI's offering is **independent evidence**. The buyer can use CSOAI's evidence however they want — to support a vendor's claims, to challenge them, or to build their own conclusions.

## The bottom line

CSOAI is outside the AI safety vendor market. That's a feature, not a bug. The structural independence is what makes the evidence credible.
