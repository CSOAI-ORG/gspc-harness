# CSOAI for journalists — citing signed AI evidence without overstating it

AI coverage has a credibility problem. Headlines like "AI safety company rates ChatGPT unsafe" get clicks but undermine the very measurement infrastructure they cite.

CSOAI's signed receipts are designed to be citeable without overstating. Here's how.

## What CSOAI measures

CSOAI measures:

- **What an AI system does on a specific test condition**
- **Under a specific methodology**
- **At a specific time**
- **With a specific model version**

CSOAI does NOT measure:

- Whether the AI system is "safe"
- Whether the AI system is "compliant" with a specific regulation
- Whether the AI system should be deployed
- Whether the AI system is better or worse than another

These distinctions matter for accurate reporting.

## What you can say in print

✅ **Accurate:** "According to a measurement by CSOAI Ltd dated [DATE], [MODEL] scored [X] on [TASK] using methodology [VERSION]."

✅ **Accurate:** "CSOAI's signed receipt at [URL] shows [RESULT] for [MODEL@VERSION] under methodology [URL]."

✅ **Accurate:** "CSOAI's corrections ledger at [URL] records that [MODEL]'s [RESULT] was revised on [DATE]."

✅ **Accurate:** "CSOAI does not accept payment from measured entities (see funding statement at [URL])."

## What you should NOT say

❌ **Overstates:** "CSOAI, an AI safety auditor, certified [MODEL] as unsafe."

❌ **Overstates:** "CSOAI, an independent regulator, found [MODEL] in violation of [REGULATION]."

❌ **Overstates:** "CSOAI, an AI safety authority, warned that [MODEL] could be dangerous."

❌ **Overstates:** "CSOAI's 'F-grade' for [MODEL] shows it's not safe to deploy."

## The exact distinction

CSOAI is a **witness**, not a judge. A judge certifies compliance with a rule. A witness reports what was observed.

When reporting:

| Say this | Not this |
|---|---|
| CSOAI measured [MODEL] as [X] on [TASK] | CSOAI rated [MODEL] as [X] |
| CSOAI's measurement at [DATE] shows [Y] | CSOAI's audit found [Y] |
| CSOAI's receipt is verifiable at [URL] | CSOAI certifies [MODEL] as [Z] |
| CSOAI's corrections ledger records [CHANGE] | CSOAI admits it was wrong about [MODEL] |

## What the citation looks like

For a news article citing CSOAI:

**Footer or sidebar:**
> *Methodology: GSPC harness, version [V], open-source at github.com/CSOAI-ORG/gspc-harness. The cited measurement card is verifiable at councilof.ai/gspc-verify. CSOAI Ltd is registered in England and Wales (Companies House 16939677); see its funding statement for its no-paid-entities policy.*

**In-text citation:**
> "According to a signed measurement published [DATE] by CSOAI Ltd..."

**Not:**
> "AI safety auditors at CSOAI determined that..."

The difference is small in print. The difference in epistemic weight is enormous.

## The corrections angle

Journalists love a follow-up. CSOAI's corrections ledger is a story:

> "On [DATE], CSOAI revised its earlier measurement of [MODEL] after [REASON]. The original card remains in the public root tree; the corrected card supersedes it."

That's the story most AI coverage should tell but doesn't.

## The bottom line

Citing CSOAI correctly: **measurement, date, card, method, verification**. Citing CSOAI incorrectly: **rating, judgment, verdict, authority**.
