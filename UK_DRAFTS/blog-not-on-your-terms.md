# What CSOAI tells AI labs that ask "why won't you measure us on our terms"

When an AI lab asks CSOAI to measure them on the lab's preferred conditions, CSOAI's answer is "no, but here's how to ask." Here's the pattern.

## The request

It happens. AI labs contact CSOAI with some version of:

- "We'll let you measure us, but only on these specific items."
- "We need to approve the methodology before you publish."
- "We want the results embargoed for [N] days before public release."
- "We want you to use our internal evaluation framework, not yours."
- "We want to fund the measurement so you can dedicate more resources."

Each of these is a request to compromise CSOAI's independence.

## Why CSOAI says no

CSOAI's structural independence requires:

1. **No lab-controlled items.** The lab can't pick easy items.
2. **No lab approval of methodology.** The lab can't soften the methodology.
3. **No embargoed releases.** The public gets the evidence immediately.
4. **No lab-provided methodology.** CSOAI's methodology is open-source.
5. **No lab funding of measurements.** No payment from measured entities.

These aren't negotiable. They're structural.

## What CSOAI says instead

CSOAI's response to the request:

> "We can't measure you on your terms. Here's what we can do:
>
> 1. Measure you on our terms. Same items, same methodology as everyone else.
> 2. Publish the result publicly. You'll see it when everyone else does.
> 3. The methodology is in our GitHub. Read it, run it, verify it.
> 4. Your signed card is yours to use however you want.
> 5. If you don't like the result, suggest corrections — we'll log them in the corrections feed.
>
> We don't take payment from measured entities. If you want to support our work, sponsor CSOAI's public infrastructure separately."

## Why the pattern works

### For the lab

The lab gets:

- Independent measurement they can cite
- A signed card they can publish (or not)
- Methodology they can audit
- Public corrections that improve future measurements

The lab doesn't get:

- Favorable conditions
- Pre-publication approval
- Embargoed release

### For CSOAI

CSOAI gets:

- Honest measurement of one more model
- One more signed card in the root tree
- One more data point for buyers and regulators
- One more demonstration that the methodology works

CSOAI doesn't get:

- Lab funding (no conflict of interest)
- Lab approval (no compromise)

### For the public

The public gets:

- An independent measurement of a major AI lab's model
- Methodology they can verify
- Corrections that improve future measurements
- Confidence that the measurement is honest

## What the lab does next

Most labs, after this exchange, do one of:

1. **Accept the terms.** They participate in the standard measurement.
2. **Walk away.** They decide they don't want independent measurement.
3. **Negotiate details that aren't core.** "Can we use a different model identifier for branding?" Often yes.
4. **Try to compromise again.** Sometimes, yes. CSOAI declines politely.

In practice, most major labs eventually participate. The labs that walk away are revealing something about themselves.

## What this teaches

The pattern teaches:

1. **Independent measurement requires independence.** Compromise breaks the product.
2. **"Yes, but" requests are red flags.** The lab that wants to control measurement isn't interested in measurement.
3. **Walking away is informative.** A lab that walks away from independent measurement is saying something.
4. **The signed card is the deliverable.** Anything else is negotiation.

## The bottom line

CSOAI measures on CSOAI's terms. The terms are public. The methodology is open. The corrections are published. That's independent measurement.
