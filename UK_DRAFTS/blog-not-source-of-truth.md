# Why CSOAI doesn't claim to be "the source of truth" for AI governance

CSOAI produces signed evidence. It is not the source of truth for AI governance. The source of truth is the regulator's interpretation of CSOAI's evidence plus other inputs.

## What "source of truth" means

A "source of truth" is a single authoritative record that everyone agrees to defer to.

In a database context: a master record is the source of truth; copies are derived from it.

In an AI governance context: a "source of truth" would be the entity whose judgments everyone treats as final.

CSOAI is NOT the source of truth for AI governance. CSOAI produces signed evidence. That's different.

## What CSOAI is

CSOAI is an **independent witness**. CSOAI produces:

- Signed cards that document specific measurements
- Public verification that anyone can perform
- Corrections feed that documents errors
- Methodology that's open-source

A witness reports what they observed. A witness doesn't make final judgments about what the observation means.

## What regulators do

Regulators take multiple inputs:

1. CSOAI's signed evidence
2. The AI developer's own claims
3. Other measurers' reports (Apollo, Metr, Gray Swan, etc.)
4. Public consultation
5. Internal review

The regulator's interpretation is the **source of truth** for what compliance means in their jurisdiction. CSOAI's evidence informs that interpretation; it doesn't replace it.

## Why CSOAI doesn't claim to be the source of truth

If CSOAI claimed to be the source of truth:

1. **CSOAI would have to defend its judgments.** Every disagreement becomes a fight.
2. **CSOAI would have to expand its scope.** "Is this AI safe?" requires deployment context; CSOAI doesn't have it.
3. **CSOAI would lose independence.** A "source of truth" has power; power corrupts.
4. **CSOAI would fail at humility.** "We have the truth" is rarely true.

By NOT claiming source-of-truth status, CSOAI preserves:

1. **Independent witness role.** CSOAI can testify without being a party.
2. **Methodology focus.** CSOAI improves the methodology; regulators interpret.
3. **Self-correction.** CSOAI publishes its own errors in the corrections feed.
4. **Humility.** "We measured what we could; here are the limitations."

## What CSOAI's evidence does

CSOAI's evidence:

- **Supports** a regulator's interpretation (independent verification)
- **Challenges** a developer's claim (third-party measurement)
- **Informs** a buyer's procurement decision (signed evidence)
- **Enables** a researcher's citation (reproducible methodology)

CSOAI's evidence doesn't **replace** the regulator, developer, buyer, or researcher. It informs them.

## What "source of truth" would require

To be the source of truth, CSOAI would need:

1. **Authority to make final judgments** — we don't have this
2. **Coverage of all AI governance questions** — we don't have this
3. **Power to enforce** — we don't have this
4. **Mandate from a regulator** — we don't have this

CSOAI's role is to provide independent evidence. The regulator's role is to interpret. The buyer's role is to decide. The researcher's role is to extend.

## The bottom line

CSOAI is a witness, not a judge. CSOAI's evidence informs decisions; it doesn't make them. That's the honest position.
