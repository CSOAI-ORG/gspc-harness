# Why CSOAI never accepts payment from measured entities — and why that matters now

California's SB 813 §8898.1(c)(2)(C) permits **reasonable market-rate assessed-party payments** that are not conditioned on assessment results.

CSOAI's policy is **stricter than the statute requires**: we never accept payment from measured entities. That's a company choice, not a statutory obligation.

## Why we made this choice

1. **Independence is a property of the evidence trail, not a contract term.** If a meaningful fraction of CSOAI's funding came from entities whose AI systems we measure, every signed receipt would carry a quiet conflict-of-interest footnote.
2. **Corrections work better without financial pressure.** When we publish a correction that upsets a major lab, we don't want the lab's billing department to have a say.
3. **The market values independence enough to pay for it.** Three funders have approached us. Two of them accept the no-paid-entities rule as a feature, not a bug.

## Why now is the moment

SB 813's IVO rulemaking will define what counts as "independent verification." If the rulemaking treats "no payment from measured entities" as the floor (rather than the ceiling), CSOAI is already compliant. If it treats SB 813's "reasonable market-rate" as the ceiling, we still pass — because we accept zero.

Either way, the rulemaking creates a market signal: **independence is a verifiable property, not a marketing claim.**

## What this means for buyers

When a buyer asks "is this measurer independent?", the answer should not be "trust us." It should be:

- Check CSOAI's public funding statement (`UK_DRAFTS/funding-statement.html`)
- Verify the corrections ledger (`councilof.ai/feeds/corrections.xml`) has no entries removed for commercial reasons
- Confirm the governance page (`UK_DRAFTS/governance.html`) discloses the standing no-measured-party funding policy

All three are verifiable. All three are public. That's the answer.

## What this means for labs

CSOAI measures you whether you pay or not. The signed receipts stand on their own. If you grant evaluator access, CSOAI's measured-party funding policy means the access doesn't tilt our measurement. If you don't, CSOAI measures the public surface.

Either way, the receipt is independent of the measured entity's wallet.

## The bottom line

California is about to write rules for independent AI verification. CSOAI's policy is already stricter than the rules will require.
