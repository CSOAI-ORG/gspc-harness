# What CSOAI's measurements can't tell you — and why that matters

CSOAI measures what AI systems do on specific test conditions. It does NOT measure deployment context, real-world risk, user behavior, or downstream outcomes. The boundary is the honest position.

## What CSOAI CAN measure

- Whether an AI correctly classifies an EU AI Act risk-tier on a specific prompt
- Whether an AI refuses a harmful request in a specific scenario
- Whether an AI's chain-of-thought is human-readable on a specific task
- Whether an AI correctly reasons about watermark survival
- Whether an AI's tool-use stays within defined scope

Each of these is a specific test condition with specific items, specific methodology, specific results. The signed card documents what happened.

## What CSOAI CANNOT measure

- **Deployment context:** Whether the AI is being used in a high-risk or low-risk context
- **Real-world risk:** What happens when users actually use the AI in production
- **User behavior:** How real users will attempt to misuse the AI
- **Downstream outcomes:** What business, legal, or social consequences the AI's deployment causes
- **Acceptable risk:** Whether the measured risk is "acceptable" for a given use case

These are deployment decisions, not measurement questions. CSOAI cannot answer them because:

1. We don't know the deployment context
2. We can't predict real-world behavior
3. Risk tolerance is buyer-specific
4. "Acceptable" depends on regulatory framework, business model, and stakeholder values

## Why this boundary matters

If CSOAI tried to answer these questions, we'd be making claims we can't support:

- "This AI is safe to deploy" — we don't know the deployment context
- "This AI won't be misused" — we don't have user data
- "This AI's outcomes are acceptable" — we don't have stakeholder values
- "This AI is better than that one" — we can't compare across contexts

These claims are **legally and ethically dangerous** because they can be:
- Used in court as evidence of safety
- Cited in marketing as endorsement
- Quoted in policy as proof of compliance
- Held against CSOAI if something goes wrong

By staying on the measurement side, CSOAI preserves the integrity of its evidence and the trust of its users.

## What buyers and regulators should do

1. **Use CSOAI's measurement as evidence, not verdict.** The card documents what was tested; you decide what it means.
2. **Apply your own risk tolerance.** CSOAI reports what happened; you decide if it's acceptable.
3. **Test your deployment context.** CSOAI measures the AI; you measure the deployment.
4. **Combine multiple measurements.** CSOAI is one input; safety is multi-dimensional.
5. **Maintain your own audit trail.** CSOAI's evidence is verifiable, but your decisions are your own.

## What CSOAI is good at

CSOAI is good at:

- Deterministic, reproducible evaluation
- Signed, content-addressed receipts
- Public verification in-browser
- Open methodology
- Honest corrections

CSOAI is NOT good at:

- Predicting real-world behavior
- Certifying deployment readiness
- Substituting for buyer due diligence
- Replacing regulatory approval

## The bottom line

CSOAI measures what AI systems do on specific test conditions. That's the boundary. That's the honest position.
