# Why CSOAI's EU AI Act work is documentation, not enforcement

CSOAI helps organisations document EU AI Act compliance. CSOAI doesn't enforce the EU AI Act. The distinction matters for credibility and scope.

## What CSOAI does

CSOAI produces signed evidence that supports EU AI Act compliance documentation:

- Risk-tier classification evidence (Article 50, Annex III)
- Transparency disclosure evidence (Article 50)
- Quality management evidence (Article 17)
- Post-market monitoring evidence (Article 72)

A buyer can use CSOAI's signed cards in their compliance documentation.

## What CSOAI doesn't do

CSOAI doesn't:

- Determine whether an AI system is "compliant" with the EU AI Act
- Issue conformity marks
- Enforce the regulation
- Represent the AI Office
- Speak for any member state

CSOAI is a measurement witness, not a regulator.

## Why this matters for credibility

If CSOAI claimed to enforce the EU AI Act:

1. CSOAI would need legal authority (it doesn't have any)
2. CSOAI would need enforcement capability (it doesn't have any)
3. CSOAI would lose its witness role (witnesses don't enforce)
4. CSOAI would lose its independence (regulators have power; witnesses don't)

By staying in the witness role, CSOAI preserves:

1. **Credibility:** CSOAI's evidence is independent of any enforcement decision
2. **Scope:** CSOAI can measure anything, regardless of regulatory status
3. **Independence:** CSOAI doesn't compete with or contradict the AI Office
4. **Longevity:** CSOAI's role survives changes in regulatory regimes

## What CSOAI's evidence does for compliance

For an organisation subject to the EU AI Act:

- CSOAI's signed cards document specific measurements
- The cards can be cited in the organisation's compliance documentation
- A regulator can verify the cards against the public root tree
- An auditor can check the methodology against CSOAI's GitHub

The cards don't replace the organisation's compliance process. They support it.

## What CSOAI's evidence doesn't do

CSOAI's cards don't:

- Certify that the AI system is compliant
- Replace the organisation's internal compliance processes
- Substitute for the regulator's enforcement decisions
- Constitute legal advice

CSOAI produces evidence. The organisation produces compliance documentation. The regulator produces enforcement decisions. The boundaries are clear.

## The general principle

The general principle: **measurers measure; enforcers enforce.**

When these roles are mixed:

- The measurer loses independence (because enforcement creates interests)
- The enforcer loses credibility (because enforcement creates bias)

When they're separated:

- The measurer produces unbiased evidence
- The enforcer makes informed decisions

CSOAI's role is to produce unbiased evidence. The AI Office's role is to make enforcement decisions.

## The bottom line

CSOAI documents; CSOAI doesn't enforce. The distinction is the credibility foundation.
