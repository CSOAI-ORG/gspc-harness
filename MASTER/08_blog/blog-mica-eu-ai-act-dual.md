# Why CSOAI matters for MiCA stablecoin compliance — and EU AI Act interaction

MiCA regulates stablecoin issuers. EU AI Act regulates AI systems. CSOAI's signed evidence layer helps issuers comply with both regimes simultaneously.

## The dual regulatory regime

A stablecoin issuer using AI for:

- Reserve management (AI optimization)
- Compliance screening (AI for AML/KYC)
- Customer service (AI chatbots)
- Risk modeling (AI for depegging risk)

Faces two regulatory regimes simultaneously:

1. **MiCA** — Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (EU 2023/1114)
2. **EU AI Act** — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689

Both require evidence. CSOAI provides it.

## What MiCA requires

MiCA requires stablecoin issuers to:

- Maintain reserves (1:1 with circulating supply for ARTs)
- Publish white papers
- Disclose reserve composition
- Submit to regulator oversight
- Implement AML/CFT controls

For AI components specifically, MiCA doesn't directly regulate the AI. But the AI's decisions affect MiCA compliance:

- AI risk modeling affects reserve sizing
- AI compliance screening affects AML/CFT
- AI customer service affects disclosure quality

CSOAI measures these AI components.

## What EU AI Act requires

EU AI Act requires:

- Risk-tier classification (Article 6)
- Conformity assessment (Article 43)
- Technical documentation (Article 11)
- Quality management (Article 17)
- Post-market monitoring (Article 72)

For stablecoin AI:

- AI for AML/CFT is likely HIGH_RISK (Annex III 6)
- AI for reserve management is likely LIMITED_RISK (transparency obligation)
- AI for customer service is likely LIMITED_RISK

CSOAI's AX-01 governance axis measures risk-tier classification. AX-15 (provenance-controls) measures reserve evidence.

## How CSOAI helps with both

CSOAI provides signed evidence that supports both regimes:

### For MiCA

- **Reserve attestation language** — CSOAI's AX-16 measures disclosure language
- **AML/KYC AI behavior** — CSOAI measures screening models
- **Reserve optimization AI** — CSOAI measures risk models

### For EU AI Act

- **Risk-tier classification** — CSOAI's AX-01 measures
- **Technical documentation** — CSOAI's signed cards ARE the documentation
- **Quality management** — CSOAI's methodology-as-code IS the quality documentation
- **Post-market monitoring** — CSOAI's corrections feed IS the monitoring

The dual regulatory burden is reduced by CSOAI's single evidence layer.

## What CSOAI doesn't do

CSOAI doesn't:

- Issue reserves (the issuer does)
- Approve MiCA compliance (the regulator does)
- Issue EU AI Act conformity marks (the AI Office does)
- Replace the issuer's compliance processes

CSOAI provides evidence. The issuer and the regulator make decisions.

## Why this matters for EU issuers

EU-based stablecoin issuers need both MiCA and EU AI Act compliance. Together:

- MiCA requires evidence of reserve and AML operations
- EU AI Act requires evidence of AI behavior
- CSOAI provides evidence for both

The combination is exactly what EU issuers need.

## What CSOAI is NOT

CSOAI is NOT:

- A MiCA compliance consultancy
- An EU AI Act certification body
- A stablecoin issuer
- A reserve manager

CSOAI is measurement infrastructure for the AI components.

MiCA + EU AI Act = dual regulatory burden. CSOAI's signed evidence layer addresses both with one infrastructure. The combination is the EU stablecoin issuer's natural compliance tool.
