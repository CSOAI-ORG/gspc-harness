# Why CSOAI's first sale was $0.02 — and why that's the right number

CSOAI's first paid measurement was $0.02. Most startups try to maximise first-sale revenue. CSOAI optimised for the first sale to exist at all.

## What $0.02 means

$0.02 is the x402-metered cost of one proof. One x402 request, one signed receipt, one verification.

That's small. Most startup founders would consider $0.02 a non-event.

## Why CSOAI celebrates it

The $0.02 isn't about the amount. It's about what it represents:

1. **Someone paid for CSOAI's measurement.** Not for CSOAI's potential, not for CSOAI's reputation — for the actual signed evidence.
2. **The x402 mechanism worked end-to-end.** A real payment on Base mainnet for a real measurement.
3. **The result was verifiable.** The buyer could verify the receipt, double-check the methodology, cite it in their own work.
4. **CSOAI's independence held.** The buyer wasn't a measured entity; the measurement was independent.
5. **The mechanism is repeatable.** Every $0.02 proves the path works; future $0.02s are the same path.

## The alternative: optimising for first-sale revenue

Many startups optimise for first-sale revenue:

- Push a "starter package" at $X/month
- Offer implementation consulting at $Y/hour
- Bundle products at $Z

This generates higher revenue. It also creates higher expectations.

CSOAI rejected this approach:

- Higher first-sale revenue implies higher ongoing commitment
- Higher commitment implies more customer touch
- More customer touch implies more influence on measurement direction
- More influence on direction compromises independence

By starting at $0.02, CSOAI's first customers are **evidence consumers**, not **measurement designers**. They buy a signed receipt; they don't get to influence the methodology.

## Why $0.02 is honest

The $0.02 reflects:

1. **The actual compute cost** of running a measurement
2. **The actual signing cost** (HSM operations are bounded)
3. **The actual storage cost** (one receipt in the root tree)
4. **The actual verification cost** (everyone can verify for free)

There is no markup. There is no "premium tier." There is no upsell.

## What CSOAI learned from the first sale

The first sale taught:

1. **The x402 endpoint works.** The 402 Payment Required → USDC transfer → verification → delivery path works end-to-end.
2. **The measurement is producible on demand.** When paid, CSOAI can run a fresh measurement and produce a new signed card.
3. **The verification is independent.** The buyer can verify the receipt without asking CSOAI.
4. **The independence held.** The buyer wasn't a measured entity.

## What's next

The next $0.02 sale will:

- Use the same mechanism (x402)
- Produce the same artifact (signed card)
- Verify the same way (public verifier)
- Cost the same ($0.02)

That's the point: the $0.02 sale is a **reproducible unit**. CSOAI doesn't need to sell fancy packages. CSOAI needs to keep selling the same thing.

## The long-term business

CSOAI's long-term revenue will come from:

- Many $0.02 sales (volume)
- Sponsorships from foundations and labs
- Enterprise evidence assembly (curated, not measure)
- Optional donations

CSOAI will not introduce:

- Premium tiers
- Concierge services
- Pay-for-priority measurement

Those compromise the product.

## The bottom line

$0.02 is the right first-sale number. It proves the mechanism works without compromising the independence.
